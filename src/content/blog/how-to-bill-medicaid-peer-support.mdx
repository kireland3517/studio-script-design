Billing Medicaid for peer support services is one of the most confusing operational challenges facing behavioral health organizations today. With more states expanding peer support coverage under Medicaid, the opportunity is real — but so is the compliance risk.

## What Makes Peer Support Billing Different

Unlike traditional clinical services, peer support is delivered by individuals with lived experience rather than licensed clinicians. This changes nearly everything about how you document, credential, and bill.

Most states require peer specialists to hold a state-recognized credential — but requirements vary dramatically. Some states accept national certifications; others require state-specific training programs. Your billing depends on having the right credential on file *before* services are rendered.

## The Core Billing Codes

Peer support services are typically billed under **H0038** (Self-Help/Peer Services) or **H2015** (Comprehensive Community Support Services). Some states have carved out their own procedure codes — always verify with your state Medicaid authority.

Key documentation elements for each claim:

- Date of service and session duration
- Location of service delivery
- Name and credential number of the peer specialist
- Progress note tied to the enrollee's treatment plan
- Supervising clinician signature (where required)

## Documentation That Will Protect You in an Audit

The most common reason peer support claims get denied or recouped is incomplete documentation. Here's what survives audit scrutiny:

1. **Treatment plan linkage** — Every session note must reference a goal in the member's current treatment plan
2. **Session-specific content** — Notes need to reflect what actually happened, not a generic template
3. **Timely completion** — Most state Medicaid programs require notes within 24–72 hours of service
4. **Supervisor co-signatures** — Know your state's supervision ratio requirements

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Billing before the peer specialist's credential is verified and on file
- Using a CPT code instead of the correct HCPCS code for peer services
- Failing to document place of service correctly (community vs. office vs. telehealth)
- Mixing peer support notes with clinical therapy documentation

Getting this right from the start protects your revenue stream and keeps your organization audit-ready.
