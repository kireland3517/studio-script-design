One of the most common compliance gaps in peer support organizations is hiring peer specialists before fully understanding what credential is required in their state — and whether that credential makes the employee Medicaid-billable.

This matters because if you submit claims for services delivered by an uncredentialed peer specialist, those claims can be recouped even if the services were genuinely delivered and documented correctly.

## Why There's No National Standard

Unlike licensed clinical professions, peer support specialist certification is not federally standardized. Each state Medicaid program defines its own credentialing requirements as a condition of reimbursement. Some states accept national certifications; many require state-specific credentials.

The result is a patchwork system where a fully credentialed peer specialist in one state may not qualify for Medicaid billing in another.

## What Most States Require

While requirements vary, most state Medicaid peer support programs require some combination of:

- **Lived experience** — a documented personal or family history of behavioral health or SUD recovery
- **Training hours** — typically 40–80 hours of approved peer support training
- **Supervised experience** — hours working under a qualified supervisor
- **Written examination** — a competency-based exam administered by the certifying body
- **Continuing education** — ongoing CEUs to maintain certification

## Highlights by Region

### Northeast
States like **New York** and **Massachusetts** have robust peer support programs with state-issued credentials. New York's Peer Specialist credential requires 46 hours of training and a supervised experience component. Massachusetts uses the CPRP (Certified Psychiatric Rehabilitation Practitioner) as one pathway but has state-specific options as well.

### Southeast
**Georgia** and **Florida** both have active state certification programs. Florida's Certified Recovery Peer Specialist (CRPS) credential is required for Medicaid billing and involves 40 hours of training plus 500 hours of supervised experience.

### Midwest
**Ohio** operates the Ohio Peer Supporter certification through the Ohio Chemical Dependency Professionals Board. **Illinois** uses the Certified Recovery Support Specialist (CRSS) credential, which requires 500 hours of experience and a written exam.

### West
**California** is still expanding its peer support Medicaid billing infrastructure under the BH-Connect waiver. **Washington** state has an established credential and has been billing peer services under Medicaid for years.

## What Organizations Need to Verify Before Hiring

Before onboarding a peer specialist in a Medicaid-billing role:

1. **Confirm the state-required credential** — Don't rely on what the candidate tells you; verify with your state Medicaid authority
2. **Obtain a copy of the credential certificate** — Keep it in the employee file with the expiration date flagged
3. **Verify the credential is current** — Many credentials expire and require renewal; a lapsed credential means unbillable services
4. **Check supervision requirements** — Some states require a licensed clinician to supervise peer specialists at a specified ratio
5. **Document verification in the HR file** — Auditors will look for this
