If your organization provides substance use disorder (SUD) treatment services and bills Medicaid, you are operating under a regulatory framework that is stricter than HIPAA. **42 CFR Part 2** — the federal regulation governing SUD records — creates consent requirements that trip up even experienced compliance teams.

## What Is 42 CFR Part 2?

42 CFR Part 2 protects the confidentiality of records held by federally assisted SUD programs. "Federally assisted" is broader than most people realize — it includes any program that receives federal funding, employs a DEA-licensed provider, or is authorized by a federal agency.

The core rule: **you cannot disclose patient records without written patient consent**, even to other providers involved in that patient's care, and even for billing purposes — with narrow exceptions.

## The 2020 Updates Changed the Landscape

The 2020 revisions to 42 CFR Part 2 aligned some consent requirements more closely with HIPAA, but critical differences remain:

- Consent must now name the *individual* or *entity* receiving the disclosure rather than just a category
- Patients may now consent to general disclosures to treating providers for treatment, payment, and operations
- Re-disclosure protections remain stricter than HIPAA

This means your consent forms need to be updated if they predate 2020 — and many organizations are still using outdated templates.

## What This Means for Billing

Billing for SUD services under Medicaid requires transmitting protected health information to the payer. Under 42 CFR Part 2, this transmission is a "disclosure" that requires either:

1. A properly executed written consent that covers disclosures to the payer for payment purposes, or
2. A court order

Your intake consent forms must explicitly authorize billing disclosures. Generic HIPAA authorizations are *not sufficient* for Part 2 programs.

## Audit Red Flags

State Medicaid agencies conducting SUD billing audits look for:

- Consent forms that don't meet Part 2 specificity requirements
- Absence of consent in the record for claims submitted
- Coordination of care records shared without proper consent
- Electronic health records that don't segregate Part 2 records from general medical records

## Practical Steps

1. **Audit your consent forms** — Do they explicitly authorize disclosures for billing and payment under 42 CFR Part 2?
2. **Review your EHR configuration** — Can you properly segregate Part 2 records?
3. **Train front desk and intake staff** — Consent must be obtained before services begin
4. **Document consent renewal** — Part 2 consents can expire or be revoked; have a tracking process

Consent management is foundational to SUD billing compliance. Getting it wrong doesn't just result in claim denials — it can trigger federal investigations.
